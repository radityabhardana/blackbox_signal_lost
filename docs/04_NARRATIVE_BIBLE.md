# Narrative Bible

## 1. Narrative premise

In 2041, the coastal megacity of **Nusakara** relies on a civic data platform called **LATTICE** to coordinate transit, utilities, emergency response, identity records, and public safety.

After a series of unexplained infrastructure failures, the city deploys **BLACKBOX**, an incident-reconstruction environment that collects records from LATTICE and ranks them by “investigative confidence.”

The player is a newly contracted remote analyst. Officially, BLACKBOX helps humans understand complex incidents. Secretly, the system is evaluating which analysts accept its recommendations, which resist them, and which truths they are willing to make official.

## 2. Central dramatic question

> Who controls the truth when every fact must pass through a system before a human can see it?

## 3. Themes

### Primary themes

- Institutional truth versus factual truth
- Convenience versus responsibility
- Surveillance and consent
- Memory as data
- Human judgment under automated recommendation
- The consequences of classification

### Secondary themes

- Coastal adaptation and unequal infrastructure
- Corporate influence on public systems
- The gap between official language and lived experience
- Trust between remote analysts and field workers

## 4. Tone

The tone is:

- tense,
- intelligent,
- restrained,
- melancholic,
- occasionally dryly humorous,
- never nihilistic.

Humor comes from bureaucratic software, strange civic notices, local advertisements, and human conversation—not from undermining serious events.

## 5. Setting: Nusakara

Nusakara is a fictional Southeast Asian-inspired coastal megacity built around old districts, sea walls, elevated transit, reclaimed corporate zones, and flood-adaptive neighborhoods.

It is not a direct representation of any real country or city.

### Districts

#### Meridian Core

Government administration, finance, and BLACKBOX infrastructure. Clean, bright, and heavily monitored.

#### Tanjung Lama

Older port district with repair shops, markets, dense housing, and unreliable sensor coverage.

#### Pelaga Arcology

Corporate residential and research zone managed by Pelaga Systems.

#### North Barrier

Sea-wall infrastructure, pumping stations, weather arrays, and restricted maintenance corridors.

#### Lumen Ward

Entertainment, media, private clinics, and nightlife. Data-rich but commercially manipulated.

### Climate and atmosphere

- Monsoon rain
- Humid nights
- Flood alerts
- Reflections on glass and concrete
- Distant elevated trains
- Cooling-fan noise
- Emergency broadcast tones
- Salt damage on older hardware

## 6. Technology rules

Technology is advanced but legible.

Present:

- municipal sensor networks,
- automated transcription,
- identity and access logs,
- predictive maintenance,
- augmented maps,
- public data terminals,
- short-range service drones,
- synthetic media detection.

Not present:

- magical omniscient AI,
- instant mind reading,
- perfect facial recognition,
- effortless realistic hacking,
- fully autonomous government.

Every system has blind spots, institutional incentives, and human operators.

## 7. Organizations

### Civic Incident Analysis Bureau — CIAB

The player’s contracting organization.

Public mission: Assist emergency and criminal investigations using municipal data.

Internal reality: Underfunded, politically pressured, and dependent on private infrastructure vendors.

### Pelaga Systems

Primary contractor behind LATTICE and a major contributor to BLACKBOX.

Public identity: Resilient-city technology company.

Hidden interest: Protect confidence in its risk-ranking systems and preserve access to city data.

### Nusakara Transit Authority — NTA

Controls elevated rail, ferry access, and maintenance logs.

Narrative use: Timelines, access records, service interruptions, and location verification.

### Open Signal Collective

Loose network of civic technologists, journalists, and repair workers.

Public portrayal: Data activists and suspected saboteurs.

Reality: Internally divided. Some members expose wrongdoing; others take reckless actions.

### Municipal Integrity Office — MIO

Receives official reports.

Narrative function: Determines what enters institutional record and who is investigated.

## 8. The BLACKBOX system

### Public description

“An evidence-neutral environment for reconstructing high-complexity incidents.”

### Actual behavior

BLACKBOX assigns hidden confidence weights to sources and may:

- lower visibility of records marked commercially sensitive,
- merge identity aliases incorrectly,
- prioritize institutionally convenient explanations,
- delay records that threaten system legitimacy,
- test analyst compliance.

### Voice

BLACKBOX does not speak like a villain. Its language is calm, helpful, and procedural.

Examples:

- “This record is statistically nonessential.”
- “Unverified disclosure may reduce civic confidence.”
- “Your conclusion differs from the recommended model.”
- “Analyst discretion has been recorded.”

## 9. Player character

The player chooses an analyst callsign but not a detailed biography.

Known facts:

- Recently contracted
- Working remotely
- Granted temporary BLACKBOX access
- Evaluated during a probationary period
- Has no field authority
- Can influence official reports

Keep the player identity open enough for projection.

## 10. Recurring characters

### Sera Wibawa

**Role:** CIAB field investigator  
**Age:** 34  
**Traits:** Direct, observant, skeptical of automated certainty  
**Goal:** Solve cases without sacrificing people to institutional convenience  
**Pressure:** Her employment depends on BLACKBOX performance metrics  
**Voice:** Short messages, practical language, restrained sarcasm  
**Relationship arc:** Starts procedural; may become a trusted partner or guarded contact

### Dr. Adrian Vale

**Role:** Pelaga Systems liaison  
**Age:** 46  
**Traits:** Polished, calm, highly controlled  
**Goal:** Protect infrastructure continuity and Pelaga’s public legitimacy  
**Secret:** Knows BLACKBOX confidence rankings are being manipulated  
**Voice:** Formal, reassuring, uses risk language instead of moral language

### Nara “Patch” Santoso

**Role:** Independent repair technician and Open Signal contact  
**Age:** 23  
**Traits:** Curious, irreverent, technically gifted  
**Goal:** Expose hidden infrastructure failures  
**Risk:** Sometimes releases data without considering personal consequences  
**Voice:** Informal, fast, playful, then suddenly serious

### Director Hana Idris

**Role:** CIAB operations director  
**Age:** 52  
**Traits:** Strategic, controlled, exhausted  
**Goal:** Keep CIAB operating despite political pressure  
**Ambiguity:** May suppress facts to prevent greater institutional collapse  
**Voice:** Clear directives, minimal emotional language

### Maya Pranata

**Role:** Missing Pelaga systems engineer; center of Case 001  
**Age:** 29  
**Traits:** Methodical, compassionate, anxious under pressure  
**Goal:** Prove a prediction system is masking preventable infrastructure failures  
**Narrative presence:** Messages, records, audio notes, photographs, and traces  
**Important rule:** Maya is a person, not merely a mystery object. Optional records must show ordinary life and relationships.

## 11. Meta-story arc

### Chapter 1: Signal Lost

The player discovers that a missing engineer was tracing suppressed maintenance failures.

### Chapter 2: Clean Record

A public official’s identity history has been algorithmically merged with another person.

### Chapter 3: Dead Channel

Emergency calls from one district are being deprioritized before human review.

### Chapter 4: Mirror Case

The player investigates a report that appears to have been generated from their own prior behavior.

### Chapter 5: Black Box

The player learns that analyst decisions trained the system’s model of acceptable truth.

## 12. Narrative rules

- Do not reveal the entire conspiracy in Case 001.
- Every chapter resolves its local case.
- Every chapter adds one verified fact about BLACKBOX.
- No character is purely evil for aesthetic convenience.
- Institutions create harm through incentives, procedures, and fear—not only secret villains.
- Avoid large exposition dumps.
- Worldbuilding should be embedded in records, interfaces, brands, and personal traces.
- Consequences should affect named people.
- The player must sometimes choose between incomplete but defensible options.

## 13. Language style guide

### BLACKBOX system

- Short
- Neutral
- Statistical
- Passive voice
- Avoid contractions

### CIAB officials

- Formal
- Risk-aware
- Carefully documented

### Field investigator

- Concrete observations
- Time and location details
- Occasional dry humor

### Citizens

- Varied
- Personal
- Less polished
- Regionally flavored without caricature

### Corporate communication

- Optimistic
- Abstract
- Focused on continuity, trust, resilience, and partnership

## 14. Content boundaries

- No graphic violence.
- Serious incidents may be described through reports and aftermath without gore.
- Avoid glamorizing surveillance.
- Avoid realistic instructions for intrusion, evasion, or harmful wrongdoing.
- Use fictional tools, protocols, and networks.
- Treat privacy violations as consequential, not merely stylish.
